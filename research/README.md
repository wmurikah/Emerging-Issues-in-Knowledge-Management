# K-FRAME Research Project

K-FRAME is being developed as an applied research instrument for studying **knowledge risk management**, **knowledge continuity**, **institutional knowledge loss**, **knowledge retention**, **decision reproducibility** and **AI knowledge readiness**.

The central research proposition is that organizations can appear mature in knowledge-management terms while still containing fragile knowledge dependencies. K-FRAME therefore studies not only whether knowledge-management capabilities exist, but whether critical knowledge remains usable, traceable and transferable when a dependency is disrupted.

## Primary research question

**Can knowledge dependency mapping and knowledge shock simulation identify material organizational knowledge risks that are not visible through conventional knowledge-management maturity assessment alone?**

## Research model

The model examines five dimensions:

1. Fragility
2. Resilience
3. Assurance
4. Memory
5. Evidence

It then derives additional measures including decision reproducibility, AI knowledge readiness and a Knowledge Loss Radius for selected shock scenarios.

## Research cycle

The intended experimental cycle is:

1. Define the process, role or knowledge domain being studied.
2. Run a baseline K-FRAME assessment.
3. Introduce a controlled knowledge shock.
4. Record predicted exposure and recommended interventions.
5. Apply one or more targeted interventions.
6. Repeat the same shock.
7. Compare model outputs with observed operational outcomes.

## Candidate study settings

The model can be tested in settings such as:

- critical role transitions;
- succession and handover exercises;
- process continuity testing;
- policy and procedure reviews;
- enterprise knowledge-base quality reviews;
- AI retrieval and enterprise copilot evaluations;
- post-incident learning reviews;
- organizational restructuring;
- repository migration or system replacement;
- knowledge-transfer programs.

## What should be measured

Possible validation measures include:

- recovery time after a simulated knowledge loss event;
- ability of a successor to reproduce a decision;
- number of critical knowledge units with no validated secondary owner;
- number of stale, duplicate or contradictory authoritative sources;
- proportion of critical decisions with traceable rationale and evidence;
- success rate of knowledge-transfer exercises;
- completeness and authorization of sources used in AI-assisted answers;
- change in process interruption after remediation.

## Data handling

Public research examples should use synthetic data or appropriately anonymized case material. Confidential organizational documents, employee information and restricted evidence should not be committed to this public repository.

The public project documents the research method. A future commercial implementation can use a private service for sensitive evidence processing, proprietary scoring, graph inference and customer-specific calibration.
