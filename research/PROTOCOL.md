# K-FRAME Research Protocol

## Working title

**Knowledge Stress Testing as an Extension of Knowledge Risk Management: Evaluating Fragility, Continuity, Decision Reproducibility and AI Knowledge Readiness with K-FRAME**

## Background

Knowledge-management programs commonly evaluate strategy, culture, governance, technology, processes and knowledge-sharing practices. These dimensions are important, but they do not always show whether critical organizational knowledge will survive a concrete disruption.

K-FRAME tests a complementary approach. It models critical knowledge as a dependency system connecting people, evidence, decisions, processes and systems, then introduces controlled shocks to study how that system responds.

## Research objective

To evaluate whether dependency-based knowledge stress testing can identify, explain and reduce material organizational knowledge risks.

## Primary research question

Can K-FRAME identify material knowledge-continuity and institutional-knowledge-loss risks that are not evident from conventional knowledge-management maturity assessment alone?

## Secondary research questions

1. Does the Knowledge Fragility Index identify concentrations of tacit or undocumented knowledge that later create transfer difficulty?
2. Does the Knowledge Loss Radius correlate with observed process interruption or recovery time under a simulated disruption?
3. Does the Decision Reproducibility Score predict whether an authorized successor can reconstruct a selected decision?
4. Do Evidence Quality and Assurance scores correlate with the proportion of current, authoritative and traceable sources used in practice?
5. Does the AI Knowledge Readiness Score correlate with the source quality, completeness and traceability of AI-assisted answers?
6. Do targeted interventions produce measurable improvement in both K-FRAME scores and observed operational outcomes?

## Unit of analysis

A study may assess one of the following:

- organization;
- business unit;
- critical process;
- critical role;
- knowledge domain;
- decision family;
- knowledge repository;
- AI-enabled knowledge workflow.

For early research, the preferred unit is a **critical process** or **critical role** because the knowledge dependencies and observed outcomes can be bounded more clearly.

## Study design

### Phase 1: Synthetic benchmark validation

Create controlled scenarios where the true weakness is known in advance. Example scenarios include:

- one-person knowledge concentration;
- documented process with missing decision rationale;
- stale but apparently authoritative procedure;
- contradictory approved sources;
- inaccessible primary repository;
- failed successor handover;
- AI retrieval from incomplete evidence.

Run K-FRAME against each scenario and evaluate whether the dominant exposure detected by the model matches the designed weakness.

### Phase 2: Exploratory organizational case studies

Select a bounded process, role or knowledge domain. Collect evidence using interviews, structured assessment, document review and process walkthroughs. Build a baseline profile and run at least one knowledge shock.

Compare the K-FRAME result with observed dependencies and practitioner judgement.

### Phase 3: Intervention study

Select one or more high-priority findings and implement targeted controls, for example:

- validate a secondary owner;
- capture decision rationale;
- resolve contradictory sources;
- assign a review date and accountable knowledge owner;
- run a successor transfer exercise;
- improve AI source provenance and access metadata.

Repeat the same K-FRAME assessment and shock after remediation.

### Phase 4: Outcome validation

Compare model change with observed outcomes such as:

- recovery time;
- decision reproduction success;
- handover completion;
- source discrepancy rate;
- escalation frequency;
- process interruption;
- AI answer provenance completeness.

## Core variables

### Predictor variables

- Knowledge Fragility Index
- Knowledge Resilience Score
- Knowledge Assurance Score
- Organizational Memory Score
- Evidence Quality Score
- Decision Reproducibility Score
- AI Knowledge Readiness Score
- Knowledge Loss Radius

### Outcome variables

Candidate observable outcomes include:

- time to restore a knowledge-dependent task after disruption;
- percentage of sampled decisions correctly reproduced by a successor;
- percentage of critical knowledge units with validated secondary ownership;
- percentage of sampled sources that are current and authoritative;
- number of unresolved contradictory sources;
- proportion of AI-assisted answers with complete source provenance;
- number of process interruptions or escalations attributable to missing knowledge;
- transfer-test pass rate.

## Data collection methods

The study may combine:

- K-FRAME structured assessment responses;
- semi-structured interviews;
- process walkthroughs;
- controlled handover exercises;
- document and repository review;
- source freshness and ownership review;
- decision reconstruction exercises;
- AI retrieval tests using approved evidence;
- post-intervention re-testing.

## Evidence classification

Every material research finding should identify its basis as one of:

- **evidence-backed:** supported directly by a reviewed source or observed test;
- **self-reported:** stated by a participant but not independently verified;
- **inferred:** derived from relationships or model logic;
- **unresolved:** contradictory or insufficient evidence remains.

This distinction is important because K-FRAME is intended to study knowledge trust as well as knowledge availability.

## Analysis approach

Early research can use descriptive and comparative analysis before attempting predictive claims.

Recommended analyses include:

1. Compare designed weakness versus detected weakness in synthetic cases.
2. Compare baseline score versus observed continuity performance.
3. Compare pre-intervention and post-intervention scores.
4. Compare score changes with observed outcome changes.
5. Examine false positives and false negatives.
6. Record cases where practitioner judgement and model output disagree.

Later studies with sufficient observations can test correlations, inter-rater reliability, construct validity and predictive validity.

## Research quality controls

- Use the same assessment version within each before-and-after comparison.
- Record the K-FRAME model version with every result.
- Separate self-reported evidence from independently observed evidence.
- Preserve the shock definition and severity when comparing baseline with re-test.
- Record any material process change that occurs between tests.
- Avoid presenting demonstration scores as validated organizational benchmarks until calibration research supports such claims.

## Ethics and confidentiality

Public case studies should use synthetic or appropriately anonymized data. Participant consent, organizational authorization and applicable research-ethics requirements should be addressed before collecting identifiable or confidential information.

Sensitive organizational evidence should remain outside the public repository.

## Expected contribution

The study aims to test whether knowledge management can be extended from capability assessment toward **knowledge stress testing**: evaluating whether critical organizational knowledge remains available, trustworthy and reproducible when dependencies fail.

A positive result would not establish that K-FRAME replaces existing knowledge-management or risk frameworks. It would support the narrower proposition that dependency-aware stress testing provides additional diagnostic information about knowledge continuity, institutional knowledge loss, decision reproducibility and AI knowledge readiness.
