## Abstract

Knowledge management is being reshaped by rapid advances in artificial intelligence, large language models, enterprise search, knowledge graphs, automation, cloud platforms, and digital collaboration. These developments create opportunities to improve knowledge discovery and reuse, but they also introduce new questions about accuracy, provenance, governance, cybersecurity, intellectual property, privacy, organizational memory, and the continuing importance of tacit human knowledge.

This repository organizes these emerging issues into a concise research resource. It focuses on the relationship between technology and organizational knowledge: how knowledge is captured, represented, shared, retained, retrieved, validated, secured, and transformed into decisions. The resource is intended for students, researchers, practitioners, information-systems professionals, knowledge managers, and organizations exploring the future of knowledge management.

## Research themes

### 1. Artificial intelligence and generative AI

AI systems increasingly support enterprise search, summarization, question answering, document classification, knowledge extraction, decision support, and knowledge creation. Their use in knowledge management raises important issues around hallucination, explainability, source attribution, model governance, bias, privacy, and human oversight.

### 2. Knowledge graphs and semantic knowledge

Knowledge graphs connect people, concepts, documents, systems, events, and organizational entities through explicit relationships. They can improve contextual search and machine-readable knowledge, while creating challenges around ontology design, data quality, maintenance, access control, and interoperability.

### 3. Digital transformation and knowledge ecosystems

Cloud platforms, collaboration tools, APIs, automation, and distributed digital workplaces have expanded the number of systems in which organizational knowledge is created. Effective knowledge management increasingly requires integration across fragmented repositories rather than dependence on a single knowledge base.

### 4. Tacit knowledge and organizational memory

A significant proportion of organizational knowledge remains experiential and difficult to document. Staff turnover, retirement, restructuring, remote work, and project-based employment can accelerate knowledge loss. Knowledge-retention approaches therefore need to combine technology with mentoring, communities of practice, structured handovers, lessons learned, and deliberate knowledge-transfer processes.

### 5. Knowledge quality, trust, and provenance

As organizations generate and retrieve information through automated systems, the ability to determine where knowledge came from becomes increasingly important. Provenance, version history, validation, authoritative sources, and evidence trails are central to trustworthy knowledge management.

### 6. Cybersecurity, privacy, and access governance

Knowledge systems often contain strategically important, commercially sensitive, or personal information. Identity and access management, data classification, encryption, retention, monitoring, third-party risk, and secure sharing are therefore part of modern knowledge-management design.

### 7. Organizational learning and knowledge sharing

Technology can make information available, but availability does not guarantee learning. Culture, incentives, leadership, psychological safety, collaboration patterns, and the usability of knowledge systems influence whether employees contribute to and reuse organizational knowledge.

## Key questions

This repository is organized around questions such as:

- How is generative AI changing knowledge creation, discovery, and reuse?
- How can organizations preserve provenance when AI systems synthesize information from many sources?
- What role can knowledge graphs play in enterprise search and semantic retrieval?
- How should organizations manage tacit knowledge that cannot be fully codified?
- How can knowledge-management systems balance openness with confidentiality and least-privilege access?
- What governance is needed when employees use AI assistants with organizational information?
- How should organizations measure whether knowledge is actually being reused and converted into better decisions?
- What happens to organizational memory when employees, platforms, and business processes change rapidly?
