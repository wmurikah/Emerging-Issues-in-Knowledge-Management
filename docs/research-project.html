K-FRAME Research Project

Can knowledge stress testing identify institutional knowledge loss, key-person risk and decision fragility that a conventional knowledge-management maturity assessment may miss?

Run the prototype Research materials

Research proposition

Knowledge can look mature and still fail under pressure.

An organization may have a knowledge base, policies, collaboration tools and formal handovers while critical work still depends on one experienced person, one inaccessible source, one undocumented exception or one stale procedure. K-FRAME studies whether those dependencies can be detected before a real disruption exposes them.

The model combines knowledge risk management, knowledge continuity, institutional memory, evidence assurance, decision reproducibility and AI knowledge readiness in a single stress-testing cycle.

Primary research question

Can dependency-based knowledge stress testing reveal material knowledge risks that conventional maturity assessment does not?

The research tests whether a baseline K-FRAME profile and a selected Knowledge Shock Simulator scenario correspond with observable outcomes such as recovery time, handover performance, source quality and the ability of a successor to reproduce an important decision.

60-second use case

One critical expert becomes unavailable on Friday.

00-05

Hook

Can the organization make the same critical decisions on Monday without the person who normally explains why the process works?

05-12

Scope

The researcher selects a critical process or role and defines the knowledge domain being studied.

12-22

Baseline

K-FRAME scores secondary ownership, documentation, traceability, freshness, consistency, transfer readiness, access continuity and AI provenance.

22-30

Shock

The researcher selects Key expert unavailable and sets the disruption severity.

30-40

Result

The tool calculates fragility, resilience, assurance, organizational memory, evidence quality, decision reproducibility, AI readiness and the Knowledge Loss Radius.

40-50

Intervention

The weakest controls drive targeted actions such as validating a second owner, recording decision rationale, resolving contradictory sources or testing a real handover.

50-57

Re-test

The same shock is repeated after remediation and compared with the baseline.

57-60

Research question

Did the measured improvement correspond with better real continuity, faster recovery and more reproducible decisions?

Study design

Baseline, shock, intervention, re-test, observed outcome.

01

Synthetic benchmark

Create controlled cases with known weaknesses such as one-person dependency, stale procedures, contradictory sources and incomplete AI context.

02

Case study

Apply K-FRAME to a bounded critical process, role or knowledge domain and compare model output with interviews, evidence review and walkthroughs.

03

Intervention

Implement specific controls that address the detected failure mode rather than generic knowledge-management recommendations.

04

Validation

Repeat the same shock and compare score changes with recovery time, handover success, decision reproduction and evidence quality.

Candidate measures

What the research can compare

Candidate validation measures include recovery time after simulated knowledge loss, percentage of sampled decisions correctly reproduced by a successor, critical knowledge without a validated secondary owner, stale or contradictory authoritative sources, knowledge-transfer test success, and the proportion of AI-assisted answers with complete and authorized source provenance.

Frequently asked questions

Knowledge risk, continuity and AI readiness

What is knowledge risk management?

Knowledge risk management examines how missing, inaccessible, concentrated, outdated or unreliable knowledge can create operational, strategic or governance risk.

What is knowledge continuity?

Knowledge continuity is the ability to preserve and transfer the knowledge required for critical work across staff changes, system failures, restructuring and other transitions.

What is institutional knowledge loss?

Institutional knowledge loss occurs when an organization loses important context, judgement, relationships, decision logic or practical know-how that had not been adequately transferred or preserved.

What is K-FRAME?

K-FRAME stands for Knowledge Fragility, Resilience, Assurance, Memory and Evidence. It is an applied research model for stress-testing organizational knowledge dependencies.

How does K-FRAME support AI readiness?

K-FRAME examines whether organizational knowledge used by AI systems is current, authorized, traceable, consistent and sufficiently complete for the intended use. It treats AI readiness as partly a knowledge-quality and knowledge-governance problem.

Open research

Reproducible where it should be, private where it must be.

The public repository documents the research rationale, study protocol, case templates and demonstration interface. Public cases should use synthetic or appropriately anonymized data. Proprietary production scoring, private graph inference and confidential organizational evidence are intentionally kept outside the public repository.
