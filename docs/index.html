Context

Knowledge management is becoming a resilience problem as well as a repository problem.

Organizations can have documentation, collaboration tools and knowledge bases while still depending on one person to explain a critical exception, one repository to recover an important record, or one stale source that silently drives decisions. Artificial intelligence makes these weaknesses more important because enterprise AI systems can retrieve organizational knowledge quickly without automatically knowing which source is current, authoritative or complete.

This research studies knowledge as a dependency system connecting people, evidence, decisions, processes and technology. The K-FRAME model then stress-tests those dependencies to examine knowledge fragility, knowledge continuity, decision reproducibility and AI knowledge readiness.

Research themes

01

Knowledge risk management

Identify where missing, stale, inaccessible or concentrated knowledge can create operational exposure.

02

Knowledge continuity

Test whether critical work can continue when an expert, role, repository, system or source becomes unavailable.

03

Institutional knowledge loss

Study how context, judgement and decision logic disappear during staff transitions and organizational change.

04

AI readiness

Evaluate whether organizational knowledge is current, authorized, traceable and suitable for AI retrieval or reasoning.

05

Knowledge graphs

Connect people, concepts, evidence, systems and decisions to reveal dependencies that isolated repositories can hide.

06

Organizational learning

Examine whether incidents, exceptions and lessons actually change future guidance, controls and decisions.
