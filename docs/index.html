Emerging Issues in Knowledge Management

How artificial intelligence, generative AI, knowledge graphs, digital transformation, organizational learning, knowledge sharing, information governance and organizational memory are reshaping the creation, discovery, protection and use of knowledge.

Abstract

Knowledge management is moving beyond the repository

Knowledge management is being reshaped by artificial intelligence, large language models, enterprise search, knowledge graphs, automation, cloud platforms and digital collaboration. These technologies can improve knowledge discovery and reuse, but they also create new questions about accuracy, provenance, governance, cybersecurity, intellectual property, privacy, organizational memory and the continuing importance of tacit human knowledge.

This project examines knowledge management as an organizational ecosystem rather than a single technology. The focus is on how knowledge is captured, represented, shared, retained, retrieved, validated, secured and converted into decisions.

Central idea: the emerging challenge is not simply how to store more information, but how to keep useful knowledge discoverable, contextual, authoritative, secure and reusable across rapidly changing people, systems and processes.
Research themes

Seven issues shaping the future of organizational knowledge

AI and generative AI

From semantic retrieval and summarization to source attribution, hallucination risk, access-aware retrieval, privacy and human oversight.

Knowledge graphs

Representing relationships between people, documents, systems and concepts to improve context, search and machine-readable knowledge.

Digital transformation

Managing organizational knowledge across fragmented cloud platforms, collaboration tools, APIs, workflows and enterprise applications.

Organizational learning

Connecting knowledge sharing, culture, communities of practice, learning loops and the practical reuse of lessons and expertise.

Knowledge quality and provenance

Preserving source, ownership, version, review status and evidence as information is copied, summarized and generated by automated systems.

Cybersecurity and access governance

Balancing discoverability with identity, least privilege, classification, privacy, secure sharing, monitoring and third-party risk.

Key questions

Questions for researchers and practitioners

  • How is generative AI changing knowledge creation, discovery and reuse?
  • How can AI-generated answers remain traceable to authoritative sources?
  • What role can knowledge graphs play in semantic enterprise search?
  • How should organizations retain tacit expertise during staff transitions?
  • How can knowledge-management systems enforce least-privilege access?
  • What governance is needed when AI assistants use internal information?
  • How should organizations identify obsolete or conflicting knowledge?
  • How can knowledge reuse and decision impact be measured meaningfully?
Practical framework

A six-layer view of modern knowledge management

Knowledge assets

What knowledge matters, where it resides and which sources are authoritative.

People and ownership

Who creates, validates, maintains, shares and uses critical organizational knowledge.

Processes

How knowledge is captured, reviewed, shared, retained, transferred and retired.

Technology

Repositories, collaboration tools, enterprise search, knowledge graphs, AI and integrations.

Governance and risk

Access, security, privacy, records management, quality, provenance and third-party exposure.

Outcomes

Whether knowledge improves continuity, learning, efficiency, innovation and decision quality.
