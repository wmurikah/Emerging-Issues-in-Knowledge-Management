Artificial Intelligence and Generative AI in Knowledge Management

AI changes knowledge management from a system that mainly stores and retrieves information into one that can classify, synthesize, explain and generate responses from organizational knowledge.

From retrieval to synthesis

Traditional enterprise search asks users to locate documents and interpret them. Generative AI can combine information from multiple sources and produce a direct response. This may reduce search effort and make large repositories easier to use, but the generated answer becomes an additional knowledge object whose accuracy and authority must be evaluated.

For knowledge-management teams, the important design question is therefore not only whether an AI assistant can produce a useful answer. It is whether the answer can be traced to current, authorized and authoritative sources.

Key opportunities

Semantic discovery

Natural-language search can help users find relevant information even when they do not know exact keywords or repository structures.

Knowledge extraction

AI can identify concepts, entities, relationships, decisions and recurring themes across large collections of documents.

Summarization and reuse

Long reports, policies and project records can be summarized for specific users, while preserving links to original sources.

Governance risks

AI can produce plausible statements that are incomplete, unsupported or wrong. It can also expose information to users who should not receive it if retrieval permissions are not enforced consistently. Other concerns include confidential prompts, external model providers, stale source material, intellectual-property restrictions, bias, weak logging and uncertainty about whether generated text is approved organizational guidance.

Useful safeguards include source citations, retrieval from approved repositories, document ownership, review dates, access-aware retrieval, logging, human review for high-impact uses and clear separation between generated suggestions and approved records.

Research questions

  • How should organizations measure the reliability of AI-generated knowledge?
  • How should retrieval-augmented generation enforce existing document permissions?
  • What provenance information should accompany generated answers?
  • When should AI-generated knowledge require human approval before reuse?
  • How should organizations manage model, vendor and data-retention risk?
