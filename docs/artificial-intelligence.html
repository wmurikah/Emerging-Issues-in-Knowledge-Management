Research note 01

Artificial Intelligence and Knowledge Management

AI is changing knowledge work from document retrieval toward contextual assistance, synthesis and machine-supported reasoning.

Overview

From repositories to intelligent knowledge environments

Artificial intelligence can support knowledge capture, classification, search, summarization, recommendation and decision support. Generative AI and large language models extend this by allowing people to interact with organizational knowledge conversationally. Retrieval-augmented generation can connect language models to controlled organizational sources rather than relying only on model memory.

The opportunity is substantial, but so are the governance requirements. AI-generated outputs may be incomplete, inaccurate or insufficiently sourced. Knowledge-management design therefore needs provenance, validation, access controls, human review and clear accountability.

Key issues

01

Knowledge discovery

Semantic search and conversational interfaces can reduce the effort required to find relevant organizational knowledge.

02

Knowledge creation

Generative systems can synthesize drafts and explanations, but generated material should not automatically become authoritative organizational knowledge.

03

Provenance

Users need to know where an answer came from, which source was used and whether the information is current.

04

Access control

AI interfaces must inherit or enforce the same confidentiality and authorization boundaries as underlying knowledge sources.

05

Human judgement

AI can augment expertise but cannot eliminate the need for contextual judgement, challenge and accountability.

06

Knowledge quality

Outdated, duplicated or poorly governed source material produces weak AI-assisted knowledge regardless of model capability.
