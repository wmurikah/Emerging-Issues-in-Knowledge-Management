Research note 03

Organizational Learning and Knowledge Sharing

Knowledge systems succeed when people can transfer experience, challenge assumptions and embed learning into everyday work.

Overview

Knowledge management is a social system, not only a technology platform.

Organizational learning depends on how individuals and teams interpret experience, share insights and change routines. Tacit knowledge is especially difficult to capture because it often resides in judgement, relationships, practice and context rather than formal documents.

Strong knowledge-sharing environments combine usable technology with psychological safety, incentives, communities of practice, mentoring, handover routines and clear ownership of important knowledge assets.

Key issues

01

Tacit knowledge

Experience and judgement require interaction, practice and reflection; they cannot always be reduced to documentation.

02

Knowledge sharing

People share more effectively when systems are easy to use and organizational incentives do not reward hoarding.

03

Institutional memory

Role transitions and employee departures can create material knowledge loss when handovers are informal.

04

Communities of practice

Peer networks help knowledge evolve through repeated discussion and application.

05

Learning loops

Lessons become organizational knowledge when they influence processes, controls, decisions and future behaviour.

06

Culture

Technology cannot compensate for cultures that discourage questions, feedback or cross-functional collaboration.
