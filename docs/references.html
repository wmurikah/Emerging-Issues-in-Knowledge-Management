Selected works framing knowledge management, knowledge risk management, knowledge continuity, institutional memory, organizational learning and responsible AI.

Knowledge management

Knowledge risk and continuity

Knowledge loss, continuity and organizational risk

Beazley, H., Boenisch, J., & Harden, D. (2003). Knowledge continuity: The new management function. Journal of Organizational Excellence, 22(3), 65-81. DOI: 10.1002/npr.10080.

Durst, S., & Henschel, T. (Eds.). (2020). Knowledge Risk Management: From Theory to Praxis. Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-030-35121-2.

These works support the distinction between storing knowledge and protecting the continuity of knowledge that organizations depend on. K-FRAME extends that problem into dependency mapping, knowledge stress testing and decision reproducibility.

Artificial intelligence

AI risk, trust and organizational readiness

Tabassi, E. (2023). Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0). National Institute of Standards and Technology, NIST AI 100-1. DOI: 10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1.

National Institute of Standards and Technology. (2024). Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile. NIST AI 600-1.

K-FRAME does not replace AI risk-management frameworks. Its narrower research contribution is to test whether the organizational knowledge available to people and AI systems is current, authorized, traceable, sufficiently complete and resilient under disruption.

Research direction

Contemporary extensions

Current knowledge-management research increasingly intersects with artificial intelligence, generative AI, semantic technologies, knowledge graphs, digital workplaces, data governance, cybersecurity, privacy and responsible AI. The K-FRAME research project adds knowledge fragility, knowledge continuity, decision reproducibility and knowledge-loss radius as testable constructs within this broader field.

This repository is a living research resource. Additional peer-reviewed references will be added as the model is tested, with preference for primary research and authoritative institutional sources.
