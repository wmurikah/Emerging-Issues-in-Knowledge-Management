Research note 02

Knowledge Graphs and Semantic Knowledge

Knowledge graphs connect entities, concepts and evidence so organizational knowledge can be discovered through relationships rather than folders alone.

Overview

Context matters as much as content.

Organizations often store information in disconnected documents, systems and team spaces. Knowledge graphs provide a way to represent people, processes, documents, systems, events and concepts as connected entities. These relationships can improve search, recommendation, impact analysis and machine-readable context.

For knowledge management, the value lies less in the graph technology itself than in disciplined semantics: shared definitions, identifiers, relationships and governance that make knowledge reusable across systems.

Key issues

01

Semantic consistency

Shared vocabularies and definitions reduce ambiguity across departments and systems.

02

Contextual search

Relationship-aware discovery can surface relevant people, documents and concepts beyond keyword matching.

03

Integration

Graphs can bridge fragmented repositories when identifiers and data quality are governed consistently.

04

Provenance

Relationships should retain source and lineage information so users can trace why a connection exists.

05

AI grounding

Structured relationships can provide additional context for AI systems and retrieval workflows.

06

Governance

Ontology ownership, change control and stewardship are essential as semantic models evolve.
