Knowledge Graphs and Semantic Knowledge Management

Knowledge graphs make relationships explicit, allowing organizations to connect people, documents, systems, concepts, projects, risks and decisions rather than treating each item as an isolated record.

Why relationships matter

Organizations often know more than their document repositories reveal. A policy may apply to a process; the process may be owned by a department; the department may rely on several systems; those systems may depend on vendors; and those vendors may introduce particular operational or security risks. A conventional folder structure rarely expresses these relationships clearly.

A knowledge graph represents entities and relationships in a form that can support semantic search, contextual navigation, impact analysis and machine-assisted reasoning.

Potential uses

Semantic enterprise search

Users can discover related concepts and records even when terminology differs across business units or systems.

Context for AI systems

Graph relationships can give retrieval and AI systems structured context beyond document similarity alone.

Impact analysis

Organizations can trace how a change to one system, process, policy or vendor may affect connected assets and responsibilities.

Governance challenges

A graph is only as trustworthy as the data and relationships it represents. Weak entity matching, inconsistent identifiers, ambiguous concepts and obsolete relationships can produce misleading results. Organizations therefore need ownership of the ontology or semantic model, data-quality rules, controlled vocabularies, lifecycle management and transparent provenance.

Access control is also important. A graph should not reveal the existence or relationships of sensitive information merely because the underlying content is stored securely elsewhere.

Research questions

  • How should enterprise ontologies evolve without becoming too complex to maintain?
  • How can knowledge graphs complement vector search and retrieval-augmented generation?
  • What controls are needed to prevent stale relationships from becoming accepted organizational knowledge?
  • How can organizations preserve authorization boundaries across graph relationships?
